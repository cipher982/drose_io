The HN discussion quickly split between two camps: those who accepted the radiation link and dug into why flight attendants fare worse than pilots (blaming more erratic schedules, circadian disruption, and less rest), and a vocal group that argued the real culprit is bleed-air contamination from engine oil and hydraulic fluids pumped into the cabin, citing “fume events” as a pervasive and underreported hazard. Several commenters pushed back hard on the bleed-air theory, pointing out that the study specifically found elevated risks only for radiation-related cancers, not for respiratory or other cancers, and that fume events are statistically rare (33 per million departements). Others called out anecdotal claims about flight attendants looking “damaged by the job” as unsupported bias, while a handful of engineers ran their own quick searches to compare flight hours between pilots and attendants, landing on about 75–100 hours per month for both.
