Don't use musl if you care about performance [comments]

94 points · 59 comments · blog.brokk.ai · 16h ago

The article is a performance analysis from a JVM-background developer who switched his Rust projects to musl for the convenience of static binaries, only to discover that musl's allocator is catastrophically slow—144% slower than glibc in his benchmarks—and that swapping in mimalloc only brings the regression down to 26% because other musl routines like memcpy and memset are also slow. The HN crowd largely agrees that musl’s design tradeoffs are well-known, but splits sharply on whether 26% is a disaster or a reasonable price for simplicity and smaller binaries; several ops-adjacent commenters push back that if you have 128GB Kubernetes hosts, just ship glibc containers and stop making the tradeoff. A deep technical tangent erupts around why compilers can't just fix slow memcpy—experts explain that glibc does runtime dispatch to a dozen microarchitecture-tuned assembly implementations that no compiler-builtin can match, while musl prioritizes tiny code size over speed. A few people make the case that if your application allocates heavily enough for the allocator to be the bottleneck, you’re already doing something wrong and should manage your own memory, which other commenters dismiss as overly dismissive of real-world workloads.
